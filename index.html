Theory & conventions
  • Time dependence ejωt; fields E = Re{Ê ejωt}; a wave travelling in +z goes as e−jkz.
  • Interface at z = 0; medium 1 in z < 0 (top), medium 2 in z > 0 (bottom); plane of incidence x–z. TE (s): E along y. TM (p): H along y.
  • εc = ε0εr − jσ/ω, η = √(μ/εc), nc = √(μr εc/ε0). Displayed n = √(μr εr) is read-only (lossless value), with the complex nc beside it when σ > 0.
  • Snell: nc1 sinθi = nc2 sinθt; kz2 = √(k2² − kx²) with the branch Im(kz2) ≤ 0 so the transmitted wave decays into medium 2; this yields the evanescent wave for θi > θc. cosθt = kz2/k2 (complex in general).
  • TE: r = (η2 cosθi − η1 cosθt)/(η2 cosθi + η1 cosθt), t = 2η2 cosθi/(η2 cosθi + η1 cosθt).
    TM: r = (η2 cosθt − η1 cosθi)/(η2 cosθt + η1 cosθi), t = 2η2 cosθi/(η2 cosθt + η1 cosθi).
    Sign convention for TM: rTM is the ratio of the tangential E amplitudes (Ex,ref/Ex,inc), so at normal incidence rTM = rTE = (η2−η1)/(η2+η1). The H-field reflection coefficient is rH = −rTM and tH = 1 + rH; tTM = η2 tH/η1 is the ratio of total E amplitudes. This is the negative of the Hecht/Born–Wolf rp. The phase readout uses this same r.
  • Power: R = |r|²; T = Re(Sz,trans)/Re(Sz,inc) with ⟨S⟩ = ½Re(Ê×Ĥ*) evaluated from the complex fields at the interface (never |t|²). For lossless medium 1 the incident/reflected interference term carries no normal flux, so R + T = 1 exactly, also when medium 2 is lossy or the transmitted wave is evanescent (then T = 0).
  • Critical angle θc = asin(n2/n1), lossless media with n1 > n2 only. Brewster angle from r = 0 in the impedance form: sin²θB,TM = (μ1/ε1 − μ2/ε2)/(μ1/ε1 − μ1ε1/ε2²), sin²θB,TE = (μ2/ε2 − μ1/ε1)/(μ2/ε2 − μ1²/(μ2ε2)); each candidate is verified numerically (|r| < 10⁻⁶). For lossy media the pseudo-Brewster angle (minimum of |rTM|) is found by a golden-section search.
  • Evanescent / attenuated transmitted wave: decay length δ = 1/|Im kz2|, shown in the HUD and as dashed envelope lines at δ, 2δ, 3δ.
  • The canvas uses a normalized free-space wavelength (λ0 = 1 unit, 8–14 wavelengths across the view); the physical λ0 = c/f appears in the HUD. Material loss uses the physical ω through σ/(ωε0).
  • Heat map: TE shows Ey, TM shows Hy (or the instantaneous |E| from Ex, Ez). Arrows: instantaneous S = E(t)×H(t) from the real fields (interference cross-terms included) or ⟨S⟩ = ½Re(E×H*).
